What we leave behind · sample

A one-page runbook, not a binder.

  1. What this site is, in one paragraph.
  2. Where the firewall, the backup, and the passwords live.
  3. Who to call, and what can wait until morning.
  4. The last change, dated, with a photo of the closet.
Business Offices that should not depend on one person’s memory. Studios, clinics, shops, and small companies. Firewalls, Wi-Fi, identity, and a closet someone else can understand. Home Houses that need signal, not another app. Whole-home Wi-Fi, a real home office, backups that restore, and cameras that stay off the family network.

One spine. Separate networks.

Work, guests, and the house should not share a password. The hardware can. The design should not.

Cables have names

Both ends. If a printer moves, the next person should not have to tone out a bundle of beige.

Backups get restored

We test a file, a mailbox, or a photo library before we call the job done. The date of that test goes in the runbook.

Passwords leave the sticky note

A shared manager for the business. A household plan that is not “ask the teenager.” Admin accounts are not daily accounts.

Approach

We will

  • Write the scope before the truck is loaded.
  • Say when the inexpensive option is the right one.
  • Photograph the closet after the work.
  • Tell you what we will not monitor.

We will not

  • Sell a rack the room does not need.
  • Install a mesh kit and call it infrastructure.
  • Leave the admin password on the modem.
  • Disappear after the invoice.

How an engagement goes.

Four steps. You can stop after the written scope if the work is not worth it.

01

Walkthrough

On site, or a careful video of the closet, the dead hallway, and the ISP bill.

02

Written scope

What we will touch, what we will not, and what it costs. No surprise line items.

03

Build

Scheduled. Labeled. The office keeps working unless we agreed on a window.

04

Handoff

Passwords in a manager, a one-page runbook, and a person who answers.
